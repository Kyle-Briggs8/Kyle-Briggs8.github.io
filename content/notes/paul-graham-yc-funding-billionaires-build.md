## ⚡ TL;DR

Paul Graham explains that Y Combinator primarily funds founders who "make something people want" by deeply understanding user needs and identifying "larval markets." - Y Combinator's core philosophy is "Make something people want," focusing on founders who deeply understand user needs and can create genuinely delightful products.
- Successful startups often begin by serving "larval markets"—small, existing groups of users with urgent needs that can grow into massive markets over time.
- The most convincing founders are "living in the future," building solutions for problems they and their peers experience, demonstrating a firsthand understanding of the market.
