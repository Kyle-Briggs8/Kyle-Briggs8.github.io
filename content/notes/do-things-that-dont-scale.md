🔗 [Do Things that Don't Scale](https://paulgraham.com/ds.html)

## ⚡ TL;DR

Startups don't grow organically; founders must actively "make them take off" by doing things that don't scale, such as manually recruiting early users and providing an "insanely great" personal experience to overcome initial fragility and gather crucial feedback.

## Summary

*Summary is AI-generated — machine voice, not mine.*

- Startups require active, often unscalable efforts from founders to gain initial momentum, rather than waiting for organic growth.
- Key unscalable actions include manually recruiting early users (e.g., "Collison installation" by Stripe, Airbnb's door-to-door efforts) and providing intensely personal service.
- Founders should focus on delighting their first users with extraordinary attention, even with an incomplete product, as this builds culture and provides invaluable feedback.
- Early-stage companies are inherently fragile; judging them by established company standards is a mistake, and founders must believe in their potential despite small initial numbers.
- The goal is to provide an "insanely great" *experience* for early users, which is critical for product development and fostering compound growth.
